# Connect the thought before changing the rhythm

Use this when a paragraph feels choppy or its short sentences merely comment on the
sentence before them. These are editorial choices, not detector features. All examples
below are constructed by an AI assistant, and their situations are fictional.

## Decide what the next sentence does

Read each sentence beside the one before it. If it supplies a reason, qualification or
consequence that belongs to the same thought, join them when that makes the relationship
easier to follow. If it only says the point matters, delete it or replace it with the
actual implication. Keep a separate sentence for a new subject, a distinct finding,
needed emphasis or a pause that prevents an overloaded clause chain.

| Relationship already supported by the text | Useful connection |
| --- | --- |
| Reason for a decision or claim | because; as when the reason is unambiguous |
| Consequence of an established fact | so; and therefore |
| Additional fact without evidence of causation | and; also, where needed |
| Qualification, concession or real contrast | although; but; a relative clause |
| No additional information | delete the sentence |

Choose the relationship before choosing the word. A conjunction can expose an inference
that the original punctuation left vague, so verify that the evidence actually supports
that inference. Joining two observational findings with "because" would invent causation.
"As" can also mean "while"; use "because" when the intended explanation would be unclear.

"Therefore" is a conjunctive adverb, so it cannot join two independent clauses with only
a comma. "The file is too large and therefore cannot be attached" works. So does "The
file is too large, so it cannot be attached." A new sentence or a semicolon can also be
grammatical, but should suit the writer's cadence.

## Worked choices

### Reason

Before: We kept the old format. There was a reason for this. Older clients still read it.

After: We kept the old format because older clients still read it.

The middle sentence added no information, while the last supplied the reason.

### Consequence

Before: The chart excludes refunds. It shows what customers originally paid.

After: The chart excludes refunds, so it shows what customers originally paid.

Use this only if the accounting definition supports that consequence.

### Measurement qualification

Before: The survey recorded intentions. It did not record appointments. This affects the claim.

After: The survey recorded intentions rather than appointments, so the result concerns
willingness to seek help.

The example assumes the survey's stated construct is willingness. In a real paper, keep
the source's terminology, attribution and citation with the claim.

### Related observations

Before: Attendance increased in April. Donations increased too. The records do not
establish a connection between the changes.

After: Attendance and donations both increased in April, although the records do not
establish a connection between the changes.

"Because" would claim a relationship the records do not establish.

### A recommendation and its explanation

Before: I can send the draft on Thursday. The team reviews submissions on Friday.

After: I can send the draft on Thursday, as the team reviews submissions on Friday.

This explains the scheduling choice without giving each clause its own beat.

### Delete the empty sentence

Before: The page lists each task beside its deadline. This is useful. It is worth noting.

After: The page lists each task beside its deadline.

Adding "because this is useful" would preserve the same empty commentary.

### Keep the useful short sentence

Before and after: I missed the deadline. I had assumed the upload window stayed open
until midnight, but the course page listed an earlier cutoff.

The opening admission has a distinct job, so joining everything would weaken it.

### Stop before the sentence becomes a chain

Before: The carrier changed the route, delaying delivery. The delivery is now due on
Tuesday. The warehouse has already packed the order. Customer support needs the updated date.

After: The delivery is now due on Tuesday because the carrier changed the route.
The warehouse has already packed the order, and customer support needs the updated date.

The supplied delay supports the cause. Two connected sentences give the reader room to
follow the delivery update and the operational response.

## Check the whole paragraph

Preserve the sequence from familiar information to the new point the reader needs.
Repeated nouns or subjects can help continuity; replacing them with synonyms can make
the thread harder to follow. Resolve vague "this" and "that" references when their
antecedents are unclear. Then check whether each pause marks a real turn in thought.
Do not force long sentences, a sentence-length distribution or a conjunction quota.

[Purdue OWL's clarity guide](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/mechanics/sentence_clarity.html)
discusses familiar-to-new information flow. [UW–Madison's transition guide](https://writing.wisc.edu/handbook/grammarandstyle/transitions/)
advises choosing connections that fit the logic and using them sparingly. These support
reader-focused editing; neither establishes a detector-bypass technique.
